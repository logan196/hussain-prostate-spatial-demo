Hussain call brief: AR → NE lineage in space (Wed Oct 14, 2026, 11:00 ET)

Live portal: see README for the URL · Script: analysis.py · Numbers: stats.json · Figures: figures/

1. Dataset (one, public, verified downloadable)

GEO GSE230282 / GSM7211257 (EHU-W3). 10x Visium CytAssist, FFPE prostate tissue from a 78-year-old man with de novo metastatic NEPC co-existing with AR-positive adenocarcinoma (ARPC) in the same section. - Paper: Watanabe et al., IJMS 2023, PMID 37240308. - I downloaded the matrix, positions, scalefactors and H&E directly from the GEO FTP (131 MB matrix). - Why this one: it is the only public, directly downloadable human spatial transcriptomics section I found with large AR-high and NE-high tumor territories side by side. That lets us measure lineage segregation, overlap, and boundaries in one tissue. - Not a perfect fit: this is de novo NEPC, not treatment-emergent, and there is no treatment history. - SHAPE cohort (Beltran lab): verified as conference abstracts only (AACR 2026 PR027 and PCF 2025/2026). It covers 25 Visium samples across adeno, mixed CRPC/NEPC and NEPC, with treatment history. No public data release found. It is the dataset that would actually fit her question.

2. Her key claims relevant here

From her Cancer Res Commun 2026 paper (corresponding author); details in hussain_profile.md: 1. Prior ADT+ARPI (HR 3.04) or chemotherapy (HR 1.75) is linked to a shorter time to t-SCNC. 2. TP53 alterations are already present in 74% of baseline adenocarcinomas. 3. RB1 loss is acquired at transformation (10% → 76% in 21 paired biopsies). 4. Paired data show clonal continuity, i.e. the NE tumor descends from the adenocarcinoma.

3. What the spatial data shows

All numbers are computed by analysis.py. - QC: 4,394 spots and 18,939 genes after QC. Median 23.3k UMI and 7.4k genes per spot. - Program scores: AR program (11 genes: AR, KLK3, KLK2, TMPRSS2, NKX3-1, FKBP5, …) and NE program (14 genes: CHGA, CHGB, SYP, ASCL1, INSM1, NCAM1, ENO2, SOX2, …), scored with scanpy. "High" means z > 1. - Program calls: 628 AR-high spots (14.3%), 890 NE-high (20.3%), and 22 dual AR+/NE+ (0.5% of all spots; 1.4% of program-high spots). - The two programs are mutually exclusive across the tissue. AR and NE scores correlate at r = −0.43. Neighborhood enrichment (squidpy, 1,000 permutations) gives z = −30.9 for AR-high next to NE-high, versus +51.5 for AR next to AR and +79.6 for NE next to NE. - They occupy separate territories: - Median distance from an NE-high spot to the nearest AR-high spot is 1.11 mm (IQR 0.82–1.52 mm; max 2.44 mm). - Only 3.7% of NE spots are within 250 µm of an AR-high spot. - Only 4 of 628 pure AR-high spots directly touch an NE-high spot. - Gradient: mean NE z rises steadily with distance from AR tissue: −0.55 at 0–100 µm, 0.27 at 0.5–1 mm, 1.19 at 1–2 mm. AR z falls in step. - Intermediate states: - The 22 dual spots form one small cluster at the AR edge (median 79 µm from AR-high). Their neighborhood self-enrichment is z = 54. - They are KLK3-high and CHGA-high but SYP-, ASCL1- and MKI67-low. That looks like adenocarcinoma with neuroendocrine differentiation, not small-cell transition. - The NE-high region by contrast is proliferative (mean log MKI67 1.43 vs 0.10 in AR-high). - Treat these as a candidate, not a proven transition zone. Verify on H&E: the cluster sits near a dark pigment/edge artifact. - Unsupervised check: Leiden clusters (12) recover the split. Clusters 0 and 4 are AR-dominant. Clusters 3 and 5 are NE-dominant (NE z 1.92 and 1.35).

Bottom line for her hypothesis: - Spatially, NE and AR states in this tumor are distinct, sharply bounded territories, not a diffuse mixture. That fits a clonal "switch" model where a lineage-converted subclone expands, rather than widespread hybrid cells. - It also means a single needle biopsy could easily sample only one state. That supports her case for paired and re-biopsy designs. - These data cannot test treatment-sequence effects: one patient, de novo NEPC, no treatment annotation, no genomics.

4. Caveats to say up front

  • n = 1 patient, one section, de novo NEPC (not t-SCNC), and no ADT/ARPI/chemo history.
  • No DNA data here, so her RB1/TP53/PTEN claims are not testable. RB1 mRNA is not reduced in NE spots, consistent with the authors' report, but mRNA is not the same as genomic loss.
  • 55-µm Visium spots contain several cells, so "dual" spots may be mixtures.
  • Thresholds (z > 1) and gene sets are choices. The script exposes --z so they can be re-run live.

5. Questions to ask her

  1. Her 21 paired biopsies: is any FFPE tissue left from baseline/t-SCNC pairs, especially mixed-histology cases? Spatial profiling (Visium HD/Xenium) of those pairs could map where RB1-loss subclones and NE programs arise, and whether ARPI exposure leaves a spatial "pre-NE" niche.
  2. In her cohort, did mixed adeno/small-cell histology at transformation behave differently from pure t-SCNC? Would she want a spatial "AR–NE distance/mixing" metric as a candidate biomarker, e.g. for a re-biopsy trigger when PSA is discordant?
  3. Would a prospective triplet-era cohort (ARASENS-like) with on-treatment biopsies be the right place to embed spatial profiling? Which endpoint matters most to her: time to t-SCNC, or response to platinum?
