- Deputy Director, Robert H. Lurie Comprehensive Cancer Center. Oversees clinical research across Lurie/Northwestern Medicine, leads the GU Oncology Program, and oversees the Translational Research in Solid Tumors and Heme Malignancies programs. - MD from Baghdad University College of Medicine. Residency in internal medicine and fellowship in hem/onc at Wayne State (fellowship finished 1989), then Wayne State faculty with VA work.
- 2002–2016 at the University of Michigan Cancer Center: Associate Director for Clinical Research and co-leader of Prostate/GU.
- Joined Northwestern in Sept 2016. ([Cancer "First Person" profile](https://acsjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cncr.32951))
- National roles: SWOG Prostate Cancer Subcommittee co-chair (1994–2012); member then chair of the FDA ODAC (2004–2008, chair 2007–08); chair of the DoD PCRP Integration Panel (2013–14); NCI Cancer Biomarker Study Section; NCI Prostate Cancer Task Force; EAB for UCLA Prostate SPORE and several NCI centers. More than 265 publications. FASCO, FACP.

## Landmark trials
- **SWOG 9916**: docetaxel + estramustine in mCRPC (NEJM 2004, Petrylak et al.). One of the two trials behind docetaxel's approval.
- **SWOG 9346**: intermittent vs continuous ADT (NEJM 2013, first author). Intermittent ADT was not shown non-inferior. The trial also established a PSA ≤0.2 ng/mL after 7 months of ADT as a strong prognostic threshold.
- **PROSPER**: enzalutamide in nmCRPC (NEJM 2018, first author).
- **PROfound**: olaparib in HRR-altered mCRPC (NEJM 2020 rPFS and OS papers, first author). First biomarker-selected targeted-therapy approval in prostate cancer.
- **ARASENS**: darolutamide + ADT + docetaxel in mHSPC (NEJM 2022, co-lead). This "triplet" design is directly relevant to her t-SCNC sequencing paper.
- Talks on sequencing: APCCC 2022 "Optimal treatment sequencing in mCRPC". Her open questions there: does same-class ARPI sequencing (abi→enza) help? How does early ARPI use in mHSPC/N+ disease change downstream response? ## Most relevant to this demo: t-SCNC in the intensified-therapy era
**"Clinical and Molecular Profiles of Treatment-Emergent Small Cell Neuroendocrine Prostate Cancers in the Era of Intensified Systemic Therapies"**, *Cancer Research Communications* (accepted Sept 28, 2026). Hussain is corresponding author. Co-authors include de Bono, VanderWeele, Hwang, and Antonarakis. ([AACR](https://aacrjournals.org/cancerrescommun/article/doi/10.1158/2767-9764.CRC-26-0370/788614/Clinical-and-Molecular-Profiles-of-Treatment), [UroToday](https://www.urotoday.com/recent-abstracts/urologic-oncology/prostate-cancer/172254-clinical-and-molecular-profiles-of-treatment-emergent-small-cell-neuroendocrine-prostate-cancers-in-the-era-of-intensified-systemic-therapies.html))
- Retrospective study across 6 centers: 140 treatment-emergent variant histologies, 126 of them t-SCNC.
- Molecular data: 23 baseline adenocarcinoma biopsies and 74 variant-histology biopsies, including **21 paired samples**.
- Therapy before variant detection: ADT alone 48%, ADT+ARPI 16%, ADT+chemo 15%, triplet 20%.
- **Prior chemotherapy (HR 1.75) and ADT+ARPI (HR 3.04) were associated with a shorter time from mHSPC to t-SCNC detection.**
- TP53 alterations were present in 74% of baseline adenocarcinomas, which is enriched compared with other HSPC cohorts.
- RB1 alterations: 76% in t-SCNC vs 10% at baseline in paired biopsies (P<0.001).
- Dual TSG loss (TP53/RB1/PTEN) in 76% and triple loss in 33%. Triple loss was associated with worse outcome (HR 2.12).
- A related earlier abstract (doublet/triplet era, 42 teSC/NEPC cases) reported:
  - 15 of 16 paired cases shared genomic alterations, i.e. clonal continuity from the adenocarcinoma.
  - RB1 alterations rose from 12.5% to 81.3%, and triple TSG loss from 31% to 100%.
  - PSA at transformation was often discordant with progression (median 0.08 ng/mL).
  - MYC amplification emerged in 25% of cases.
